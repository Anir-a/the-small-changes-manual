# Editorial critique and revision

## Diagnosis

The previous edition was a useful illustrated guide, but it was not yet an engaging short story. Alex illustrated recommendations without a specific desire, lived detail or relationship. The page quickly switched from a three-panel anecdote into a wellness interface. Repeated disclaimers and labels disrupted the rhythm. Generic headings could be swapped between self-help sites without changing their meaning.

## Revision

- A specific opening problem: a demanding plan postponed to Monday while an ordinary Tuesday slips away.
- A recurring physical detail: the kettle and the reachable phone frame the opening and ending.
- A concrete work scene: a missing sentence becomes three rough sentences, not a productivity miracle.
- A relationship with a named fictional character, Maya, and a small moment of missed attention.
- A setback after the first useful day. Alex misses the routine twice, then uses the next chance.
- An ending shown through a decision, with no dramatic health transformation.
- Scientific explanations live in optional notes directly beside the relevant scene. They are not presented as causes proven by the fictional outcomes.
- The chooser and plan follow the story. Full references, care limits and rights details remain available without interrupting every paragraph.

## Integrity limits

The story is original fiction. Its events do not establish efficacy. No new clinical claims were added. The existing source set and research limits are retained. The earlier 35-page PDF is labelled as a longer companion; it is not represented as the new short story. This revision aims for editorial quality and reader engagement, not a prediction or claim of bestseller status.
